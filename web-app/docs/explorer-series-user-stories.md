# Explorer Series: how the two new pages work

A plain-language walkthrough of the host application page and the Council's
review page. No technical detail — this is the story of what people actually do.

---

## Page 1 — "Apply to Host an Explorer Event"

**Who it's for:** a tournament organiser who wants to run an Explorer Series
event at their local game store.

### Meet Ruben

Ruben runs Sorcery nights at Waterloo Games in Mechanicsville, Virginia. He
hears the Explorer Series is taking applications for 2027.

**He finds the page.** There's an "Apply to Host Your Own Explorer Event"
button on the Community Series page, right under the description of the Series.
It's also in the site menu.

**He signs in.** He can use Discord or Google — whichever he already has. We
ask him to sign in for one reason: so we know the application is from a real
person and so he can come back to it later.

**He fills out the form.** It's the same questions as the old Google Form,
grouped into sections so it doesn't feel like a wall of text:

- About you — name, email, Discord handle
- Where you play — city, state, country
- Your local game store — which store, its page on SorceryTCG.com, and whether
  the owner has agreed
- The event — how many players he expects, three dates he'd like
- Your experience — how many events he's run and what they were like, and why
  he wants to host
- Anything else — a reference, other things we should know, other organisers we
  should talk to

Before he can submit, he ticks a box confirming he's read the Navigator
Essentials page, which is linked twice on the form.

**He submits.** He gets a confirmation that tells him what happens next: the
Council reviews applications in batches and will get back to him in late
November. If he needs help, there's a link to the Explorer Series Discord.

**He changes his mind about a date.** He comes back to the same page. Instead
of a blank form, he sees his own answers, filled in, with a note saying he's
already applied and what the status is. He edits the dates, hits "Update
Application", and gets a confirmation. He can keep doing this right up until
the Council makes a decision — after that the page becomes read-only, because
the answers are the record of what was decided.

### The short version

> As an organiser, I can apply to host an event in one sitting, and fix my
> answers afterwards without emailing anyone.

## Page 2 — The Council's review page

**Who it's for:** Explorer Series Council members reviewing applications.
Only they can see it.

### Meet the Council

**They hear about it immediately.** The moment Ruben submits, every Council
member gets a Discord DM: who applied, where they are, their store, and a link
straight to the review page. Nobody has to keep checking.

**They see the whole field at a glance.** The page opens on a map of every
application, with a dot for each one, colour-coded by where it stands —
waiting, pre-approved, approved, or turned down. Underneath, a card for each
applicant showing their name, town, store, how many Council members have
scored them and their average so far.

**They open Ruben's application.** Everything he wrote, in order. Plus
something he didn't write: we look up his store on SorceryTCG.com and show what
it *actually* drew over the last nine months — the median headcount and a graph
of every event, so the Council can compare "he says 25–32" against the real
numbers. If the lookup can't find the store, there's a button to try again.

**They score him.** Three ratings, one to five:

- Enthusiasm
- Track record of running events
- History of local player activity

Each Council member scores independently and can change their mind later. The
page shows everyone's scores side by side and the running average. Leaving a
rating blank is fine — it just doesn't count toward the average, rather than
dragging it down.

**They talk it over.** Notes on the application, visible to the rest of the
Council. "Spoke to the store owner, they're in."

**They decide.** Someone sets the application to pre-approved, approved or
rejected. This is always a human decision — nothing is ever decided
automatically by the scores.

**They take it elsewhere.** One button exports everything — applicants, scores,
averages, store numbers — as a spreadsheet.

### Two extras

**Adding someone who hasn't applied.** The Council knows organisers who'd be
great but haven't applied yet. They can add them by name and Discord handle, and
score and discuss them like anyone else.

**The events map.** There's a switch on the review page that puts a map of past
Explorer events on the public Community Series page, above the standings — dots
sized by how many players turned up. It's off until the Council switches it on,
so it never appears half-finished.

### The short version

> As a Council member, I hear about every application straight away, see the
> field on a map, judge each organiser against what their store really draws,
> and score and decide alongside the rest of the Council in one place.

---

## What we deliberately did not do

**Nothing is auto-approved or auto-rejected.** The scores inform the Council;
they don't decide for it.

**Applications are never lost to a technical hiccup.** If Discord is down, or
a store page can't be reached, or a town can't be found on the map, the
application still goes through. Those are all retried or fixed afterwards.

---

## Still open

- **A waitlist.** Organisers who aren't ready to apply this early need
  somewhere to land. Today a Council member can add them by hand, but if that
  person later applies properly, we end up with two separate records of them
  and the Council's early notes don't carry across.
